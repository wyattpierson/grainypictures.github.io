Anyway, as I was saying, your book was the onlyu useful thing I could find. I was wondering is you could answer a few questions. I tried the answer archive, but I think I misunderstood the concept.

  1. Could you recommend some good sources?
  2. What advantages are there for independent filmakers?
  3. Are ther advantages because of disadvantages?
  4. How do festivals help in establishing a filmaker?
  5. How would a filmaker finance their film?

I know these sound like dumb surface questions, but I'm a high school student in Arkansas and film isn't one of the main interests here. If you could reply, i'd be greatly in your debt, thank you.



Janet Replies:

1. Could you recommend some good sources?

Go to our links area and check out some of the sites: Filmmaker Magazine, AIVF/The Independent, IndieWire, etc. -- and then check out their links. There are books written by filmmakers about their experiences, most notably Spike Lee about the making of "She's Gotta Have It" and Robert Rodriquez's "Rebel Without a Crew." Go to Amazon and/or Barnes & Noble online and research "independent filmmaking" for more sources.


2. What advantages are there for independent filmakers?

Ability to do what you want on your terms.


3.Are ther advantages because of disadvantages?

I misread this at first and thought it was just asking for the disadvantages (see below) but it's more sophisticated than that. The disadvantage is that you're not working for hire - therefore you theoretically have more freedom. Yet you still need money - so you're not really free. I don't know. Some people subscribe to : "In limitation there is freedom." The straight disadvantage: Filmmaking is very expensive - and the money has to come from somewhere. Working independently (unless you're independently wealthy) means spending time raising money, and generally working with much less than a better capitalized entity could supply. Also, there's no guarantee that after you make your film that anyone in the world will want to see it


4. How do festivals help in establishing a filmaker?

Festivals help by acting as curators. If you're accepted in an festival, it means that someone likes your film. Festivals allow a filmmaker's work to be seen by a general audience, press, fimmakers and others in the film business. Festival attendees (including many in the business) are actively looking for work to get excited about.


5. How would a filmaker finance their film?

There are detailed answers to this in "Spike, Mike." Lots of people raise money from their friends and family. Michael Moore used bingo games. Some filmmakers use foreign pre-sales. Some use funds raised by commercial work. Many go into credit card debt. There are a million answers to this question with one commonality - it's hard to do! Go back to your source material.

Hope this helps.

Janet Pierson
